Before you start

How This Actually Goes.

The real sequence, the real costs, and the parts that catch people off guard — specific to this market, not a national template.

1

Talk to a lender before you talk to us

A pre-approval letter tells you what you can actually spend and tells a seller you're serious. In a market this size, sellers routinely take a slightly lower offer from a pre-approved buyer over a higher one without a letter. Local lenders tend to close faster here than national call centers, and we can point you to several without taking anything for the referral.

2

Decide which side of the line you're open to

Fort Smith sits on the state border, and the same money buys differently in Pocola or Poteau than it does in Chaffee Crossing. Property taxes, school districts, and insurance all shift when you cross. We're licensed in both states, so it costs you nothing to look at both — but it's worth deciding early, because it changes which loan programs fit.

3

See houses in person, quickly

Photos hide layout. A house that looks open online can be chopped into small rooms, and a listing with mediocre photos is sometimes the best house on the street. Once you know your range, seeing six houses in an afternoon teaches you more than a month of scrolling.

4

Make the offer, then protect yourself in the inspection window

An offer is more than a number — closing date, what conveys, who pays what, and how long you have to inspect all matter. The inspection period is the part to take seriously. Older housing stock here often has original wiring behind a renovated kitchen, and that's exactly the kind of thing that decides whether a house can be financed conventionally or not.

5

Closing

Title work, appraisal, final walkthrough, then signing. Budget for closing costs beyond your down payment — commonly two to five percent of the purchase price, though it varies with your loan and what's negotiated. We'll give you a written estimate well before you're at the table, not the day of.

1

Get a real number, not an automated one

Online estimates work where houses are interchangeable. Most of this market isn't — acreage, shops, additions, and homes with no true comparable are the norm rather than the exception. Someone needs to walk your property. That visit is free and there's no obligation attached to it.

2

Fix the things that shrink your buyer pool

Not everything needs fixing. But some conditions cut out entire categories of financing — and when FHA, VA, and USDA buyers can't qualify on your house, you're left with conventional and cash, which is a much smaller pool. We'll tell you which repairs pay for themselves and which ones don't.

3

Photography is not the place to save money

The photos are the showing. Buyers decide in seconds whether to click, and phone snapshots in bad light lose that decision before anyone walks through the door. Professional photos, and video where the property warrants it, are the single highest-return thing you can do on a listing.

4

Price it for the first two weeks

Listings get their most attention right after they hit the market. Priced correctly, that's when offers come. Priced high with a plan to reduce later, you spend that window on the wrong buyers and end up negotiating from a weaker position with a stale days-on-market number attached to the listing.

5

Offer, inspection, closing

When an offer arrives you'll get it broken down plainly — what you net, what the contingencies are, and where the risk sits — not just a number and a signature line. Inspection findings are usually negotiable rather than fatal. Then title work, appraisal, and closing.

None of this is legal or tax advice. Every property and every situation is different, and the numbers above are general to this market rather than specific to your house. Call the office and we'll walk through yours.
